## The Opportunity

**No agent-facing land CMA tool exists anywhere.** Every CMA tool on the market (Cloud CMA, RPR, HouseCanary) is built for houses. The land-specific tools (PRYCD, Price Boss, Land Insights) serve wholesale land investors, not listing agents. This is a wide-open competitive gap.

## How Land Valuation Differs from Residential

### Professional Appraisal Methods

1. **Sales Comparison Approach** (primary method) - Find recent sales of similar parcels, adjust for differences. Challenge: vacant land sales are infrequent and parcels are highly heterogeneous. In WNC, may need 12-24 months of data and 10-20 mile radius to find enough comps.

2. **Extraction/Abstraction Method** - When vacant land comps are scarce, use improved property sales and subtract depreciated replacement cost of improvements. Remainder = implied land value. Formula: Sale Price - Depreciated Replacement Cost = Land Value.

3. **Allocation Method** - Assigns percentage of total property value to land vs. improvements based on new construction patterns. Less reliable in rural areas.

4. **Income Approach** - For land generating income (timber, farm leases, mineral royalties, hunting leases). Most applicable to 50+ acre tracts with productive timber.

5. **Subdivision Development Method** - For tracts where highest-and-best use is subdivision. Estimate total lot sale revenue, subtract development costs, discount for absorption time. Powerful for parcels with subdivision potential.

### Highest and Best Use Analysis (Foundation of All Valuation)

Tests potential uses against:
1. Legally permissible (zoning, deed restrictions, regulations)
2. Physically possible (topography, soils, access, utilities)
3. Financially feasible (will the use generate sufficient return?)

## Key Value Drivers for Rural Mountain Land (Priority Order)

### 1. Road Access (Single Biggest Value Driver)
- Paved, state-maintained road frontage: Maximum value, enables subdivision. 2-4x over landlocked.
- Gravel, state-maintained: Still strong.
- Gravel, private (shared maintenance): 10-20% discount. Buyers worry about maintenance disputes.
- 4WD-only / seasonal: 30-50%+ discount. Limits to recreation-only buyers.
- Landlocked (no deeded access): Essentially unsellable. No lender will finance.
- Paved vs. dirt road: ~15-25% premium for paved.
- Long road frontage: Additional value (enables future subdivision).

### 2. Topography and Buildability
- Level to gentle slope (<10%): Maximum value.
- Moderate slope (10-20%): Buildable but costs rise. Foundation, grading, retaining walls.
- Steep slope (20-30%): Significant cost increase. NC classifies as "provisionally suitable" for septic. 10-30% discount.
- Very steep (>30%): Extremely expensive. NC classifies as "unsuitable" for septic. Often unbuildable.
- Grading costs: $1-$15/cubic yard, typical residential $1K-$5K for gentle slopes, much more for steep.

### 3. Utilities
- Power at road: 10-20% premium vs. no power.
- Power extension: $15K-$30K per mile. Each mile reduces premium.
- Public water: Significant value-add, eliminates well risk.
- Well required: $30-$60/ft in NC, 100-500 feet deep typical. No guarantee of adequate flow.
- Public sewer: Rare in rural WNC but extremely valuable.
- Septic required: Value depends on soil evaluation status.
- Approved improvement permit: MAJOR value-add. Removes biggest buildability risk.
- Failed evaluation: Land may be unbuildable. Engineered systems cost $15K-$40K+.
- High-speed internet: Starlink reduced this gap but fiber still commands premium.
- Cell service: Impacts livability and safety.

### 4. Views
- No view / wooded: Baseline.
- Partial or seasonal: 5-15% premium.
- Panoramic mountain views: 25-75%+ premium. Single most emotionally compelling feature in WNC.
- Unobstructed, stunning: Can reach 75-100% over comparable parcels.
- Views are subjective and hard to adjust precisely. Paired sales analysis is the best tool.

### 5. Water Features
- Dry land: Baseline.
- Seasonal creek: 10-20% premium.
- Year-round creek: 25-50% premium.
- River frontage: 25-75% depending on river, flow, usability.
- Pond or spring: 15-30% premium. Springs highly valued for water supply.
- Must compare type-for-type. Also assess: frontage length, water quality, flood risk.

### 6. Septic Feasibility (NC-Specific)
- NC no longer uses simple "perc test." County health departments do comprehensive site evaluations.
- Approved improvement permit on file: Major value-add.
- No evaluation done: 10-30% discount (buyer assumes risk).
- Failed evaluation: Steep discount or nearly unsaleable for residential.
- NC slopes 15-30%: "Provisionally suitable." Above 30%: "Unsuitable."

### 7. Zoning and Restrictions
- Unrestricted (no HOA, no covenants): Premium in WNC. Allows manufactured homes, multiple structures, STRs, agriculture, small business.
- Light restrictions: Sometimes positive (protects values).
- HOA with architectural review: Mixed impact.
- Conservation easement: Reduces value 25-60%. Tax benefits partially offset.

### 8. Flood Zone Status
- Zone X: No flood insurance required. Full value.
- Zone AE/A (100-year floodplain): 4-21% value reduction. Requires flood insurance if financed.
- Partially in floodplain: Value based on usable non-flood acreage.

### 9. Acreage Scaling (Plattage Effect)
Price per acre does NOT scale linearly. Larger tracts = lower $/acre.
- 1-5 acres: Highest $/acre (residential building lots)
- 5-20 acres: Moderate (small homestead tracts)
- 20-50 acres: Noticeable decline
- 50-100 acres: Further decline, limited buyer pool
- 100+ acres: Lowest $/acre, extended market time

### 10. Other Factors
- Distance to towns/amenities: Within 10-15 min = full value. 45+ min = significant discount.
- Elevation: WNC sweet spot is 2,500-4,000 ft. Above 4,000 = harsh winters. Below 2,500 = fewer views.
- Timber value: Appalachian hardwood $2K-$8K/acre for mature stands. Black walnut, white oak, cherry are highest value.
- Mineral rights: Severed mineral rights reduce value and buyer pool.
- Easements: Pipeline or power line through middle reduces usability. Access easements across neighbors reduce privacy.
- Subdivision potential: Long paved road frontage enables division into smaller, higher $/acre lots.

## Paired Sales Analysis for Land

**Process:**
1. Find two sales similar in all respects except the feature to measure.
   - Sale A: 10 ac, paved road, power, gentle slope, NO creek. $80K ($8K/ac).
   - Sale B: 11 ac, paved road, power, gentle slope, WITH creek. $132K ($12K/ac).
   - Creek premium: $4K/acre (~50%).
2. Validate with 3-5 additional pairs isolating the same feature.

**Challenges in rural mountain markets:**
- Finding truly comparable pairs is very difficult.
- May need to pair sales differing in more than one feature.
- One pair is insufficient. Need 3-5 for confidence.
- Time adjustments also derived from paired sales (same property, different sale dates).

## WNC Land Market Data by County

### Macon County (Franklin)
- Average $/acre: ~$81,494 (county-wide), ~$53,185 (Franklin Township)
- Unrestricted land average: ~$39,564/acre
- Active listings: ~533-549
- 2023 revaluation: Total taxable value jumped 57.5% to $12.6B. Tax rate dropped from $0.40 to $0.27.
- Character: Farther from Asheville (~65 mi), more homestead/agricultural buyers.

### Jackson County (Sylva / Cashiers)
- Median $/acre: ~$14,821 (county-wide)
- Average listing $/acre: ~$72,300 (skewed by Cashiers)
- Unrestricted land average: ~$17,153/acre
- Active listings: ~842
- EXTREME split market: Sylva = modest mountain prices. Cashiers/Highlands plateau = luxury market ($463K/acre average listing).
- Cashiers/Highlands: Avg listing price ~$1.5M. Atlanta/Charlotte/FL summer retreat buyers.

### Haywood County (Waynesville / Maggie Valley)
- Median $/acre: ~$17,763
- Undeveloped land median: ~$16,492/acre
- Small lots (<1 acre) in Maggie Valley: $30K-$62K/acre
- Tax rate: $0.55 per $100 (highest of four counties)

### Swain County (Bryson City)
- Average listing price: ~$537K (avg lot size 35 acres)
- Implied average: ~$15,340/acre
- Small lots near town: $144K+/acre
- ~86% of county is federal land (GSMNP, Nantahala NF, Cherokee). Severe supply constraint.

### Why Prices Vary

## Free Data Sources with APIs

### USGS 3DEP (Elevation/Slope/Viewshed)
- 1-meter resolution LiDAR-based DEMs for WNC
- Can compute: slope maps (% grade per cell), aspect maps, buildability scores
- **Viewshed analysis possible**: Given a point on a parcel + DEM, calculate line-of-sight to determine view quality programmatically
- Access: National Map download portal, AWS Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF, OpenTopography API, Python `py3dep` library
- Tools: QGIS Visibility Analysis plugin, GRASS GIS r.viewshed, xarray-spatial

### USDA Web Soil Survey (Soil/Septic)
- websoilsurvey.nrcs.usda.gov
- SSURGO database covers 95%+ of US counties
- Provides: soil type, drainage, depth to bedrock, water table depth, septic suitability ratings, building site development ratings
- Programmatic access: Soil Data Access web service at sdmdataaccess.nrcs.usda.gov (REST + WFS, supports SQL queries)

### FEMA Flood Maps
- National Flood Hazard Layer (NFHL) available as GIS web services (WMS/WFS)
- Downloadable GIS files by county/state
- National Flood Data API for address/polygon flood zone lookup
- NC-specific: flood.nc.gov

### FCC Broadband/Cell Coverage
- broadbandmap.fcc.gov - broadband by ISP
- FCC Mobile LTE Coverage Map - 4G LTE from major carriers
- CoverageMap.com API with Mapbox Vector Tiles

### NC Landslide Hazard Viewer
- NC Geological Survey + NEMAC (UNC Asheville)
- 4,500+ documented landslides including 2,000+ from Hurricane Helene
- Includes: landslide points, outlines, deposits, hazard ranking

### County GIS (Already Integrated)
- Jackson County: Best API (beds, baths, sqft, year built)
- Haywood County: Good API (sqft, year built, acreage)
- Swain County: Basic data
- Macon County: API currently broken (500 errors)
- NC OneMap: Statewide fallback

## Competitive Landscape

### Existing Tools (All Miss the Mark for Agents)

| Tool | Target User | Land Support | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cloud CMA | Listing agents | Zero land features | Houses only |
| RPR | Agents | Basic, AVM fails on land | No land-specific adjustments |
| HouseCanary | Institutional | AVM trained on improved properties | Not for agents |
| PRYCD | Land investors | County/geo pricing models | Wholesale deals, not listing presentations |
| Price Boss | Land investors | Data-driven, under-$50K focus | Not for agents |
| Land Insights | Land investors | AI comping, $4.5K-$5K/year | Wrong audience |
| AcreValue | Ag land | Excellent for farmland | Agriculture-focused, not mountain/recreational |
| AgriData Surety Pro | Rural appraisers | Soil/aerial maps | Midwest/Plains, not Appalachian |

### The Gap
Nobody serves the listing agent who needs to:
1. Pull land-specific comps with meaningful filters
2. Integrate GIS data (slope, soil, flood, views) into the analysis
3. Use a structured land adjustment grid
4. Generate a professional PDF for seller meetings
5. Calibrate to local mountain markets

## Proposed Land CMA Adjustment Categories

### Physical Characteristics
- Acreage (with non-linear $/acre scaling curve)
- Usable/buildable acreage percentage
- Slope analysis (from DEM data)
- Elevation

### Access & Infrastructure
- Road type (paved state / gravel county / private gravel / 4WD / landlocked)
- Road frontage (linear feet)
- Power availability and distance to nearest pole
- Water (public / well required / well exists / spring)
- Septic status (approved permit / evaluation needed / failed / public sewer)
- Internet/cell coverage

### Natural Features
- View quality (with potential automated viewshed analysis)
- Water features (none / seasonal creek / year-round creek / river / spring / pond)
- Timber value estimate
- Soil quality summary from SSURGO

### Legal & Market
- Zoning/restrictions status (unrestricted / light covenants / HOA / conservation easement)
- Flood zone (X / AE / A)
- Landslide hazard ranking
- Mineral rights status (intact / severed)
- Subdivision potential
- Time adjustment (market appreciation/depreciation)
