### SLIDE 5: Professional Photography

"For us, it all starts with professional photography. And here's why this matters more than most people realize."

**The data:**
- Listings with professional photos sell **32% faster**, averaging 89 days on market vs. 123 days for cell phone photos
  *(Source: Real Estate Exposures, Average Days on Market Study)*

- Homes with professional photography command a **47% higher asking price per square foot**
  *(Source: Redfin / PR Newswire analysis)*

- Listings with professional photos receive **118% more online views**
  *(Source: Redfin study)*

- Professional photo listings close between **$934 and $116,076 higher** than comparable properties with low-quality images
  *(Source: PhotoUp / Redfin data analysis)*

"It blows my mind that over half of all listings in our MLS are photographed with a cell phone. The camera on your phone is fine for texting your friends, but it's not going to capture the lighting, angles, and composition that makes a buyer stop scrolling and click on your listing. The first showing happens online. If the pictures don't stop someone mid-scroll, they're never coming to see your home in person."

"We hire a professional real estate photographer. That's all they do. We'll take every room with multiple lighting setups because every room in your house has different light. We typically shoot 100-250 photos and select the best 25-40 for the listing."

### SLIDE 13: Getting Your Home Ready

"Should you decide to list with me, we're going to go through a room-by-room checklist of everything to do to get your house market-ready without blowing your budget."

"Most of the time it's cleaning, minor staging with your own furniture, and small touch-ups. Nothing major for a house in good condition. You'll get this as a detailed email checklist you can print out and work through room by room."

**On staging (if applicable):**
- Staged homes see a **1-10% increase in offer price**
  *(Source: 2025 NAR Profile of Home Staging)*

- **49% of seller's agents** observed that staging reduced time on market
  *(Source: NAR 2025 Home Staging Report)*

- Most important rooms to stage: **Living room (37%), Primary bedroom (34%), Kitchen (23%)**
  *(Source: NAR)*

"For vacant homes in good condition, I usually recommend professional staging. Vacant homes just don't photograph or show as well. For homes where the family is living there, we typically work with what you have and just optimize the arrangement."

### SLIDE 14: The Schedule

"You told me you'd like to be on the market by [date]. Let me work backwards from there."

**Coming Soon Period (1 week):**
"We do a 'coming soon' listing where everyone can see your property but can't get in yet. This compresses our days on market because buyers have a full week to set up showings for day one. Instead of showings trickling in over two weeks, we often have 10-20 showings lined up for launch day."

**Marketing Prep (1 week before coming soon):**
"We need at least a week for professional photos, video production, drone footage, floor plans, postcard printing, online advertising setup, and website creation."

**Your Prep (varies):**
"Then we figure out how much time you need to complete the room-by-room checklist. That gives us our start date."

### SLIDE 15: Pricing Your Home

"Now let's talk about what your home is worth."

**The data on pricing right:**
- After **14 days on market**, the number of views on your listing dramatically decreases
  *(Source: Zillow / Redfin market data)*

- A home listed within **1% of the final selling price** has a **50% chance** of going under contract within 1-14 days
  *(Source: HomeLight study)*

- Homes that require a price reduction spend a median of **23 days at the original price** before reducing, then another **12 days** to go under contract. Homes priced right go under contract in a median of **5 days**
  *(Source: Redfin / HomeLight data)*

"The best chance to sell your home for the most money is right after it gets listed. We really want to sell in that first 14-day window, and the only way to do that is to price it right from the start."

[Pull up comps on laptop, go through one by one]

"Let's look at comparable sales. Every home is unique, but comps give us a range and then we adjust based on how your home compares."

[After each comp]: "Where do you think your house rates compared to this one?"
[After all comps]: "Based on everything we just looked at, where do you think your house should fall in that range?"
