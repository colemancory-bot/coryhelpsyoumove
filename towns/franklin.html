Franklin, North Carolina

The Gem Capital of the World — where the Appalachian Trail meets small-town Southern hospitality.

Why People Love Franklin

Franklin is the county seat of Macon County and proudly calls itself the "Gem Capital of the World" — a nod to the area's rich mineral heritage and the many gem mines still open to visitors today. The town's historic downtown features a charming clock tower, local boutiques, and a growing restaurant scene that reflects both its Appalachian roots and its increasingly diverse community.

The Appalachian Trail passes nearby, and the Nantahala National Forest surrounds the town on nearly every side. Franklin offers a lower cost of living compared to neighboring Highlands and Cashiers while still providing access to the same stunning natural beauty. The Little Tennessee River Greenway provides walking and biking trails right through town.

Real Estate in Franklin

Franklin consistently offers some of the best value in Western NC. The market is diverse — from in-town homes within walking distance of shops and restaurants, to mountain properties with long-range views, to unrestricted acreage perfect for homesteading or hobby farming. The Otto and Burningtown areas south and west of town are especially popular for larger parcels.

Entry-level homes start in the $200K–$350K range, with mid-range properties from $350K–$600K. Luxury homes and estates with acreage can be found from $600K–$1.2M+. Land is among the most affordable in the region, making Franklin a popular choice for people looking to build.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Franklin Real Estate

What is the median home price in Franklin, NC?

Franklin and Macon County offer some of the most affordable real estate in Western North Carolina. The average land price is approximately $153,000, and homes range from under $200,000 for older properties to $500,000+ for larger homes with acreage. The area is particularly known for affordable large-acreage parcels. Contact Cory Coleman for current pricing.

Why is Franklin called the "Gem Capital of the World"?

Franklin earned this nickname due to the rich mineral deposits found in Macon County. The area has numerous gem mines where visitors can search for rubies, sapphires, garnets, and other gems. The annual Macon County Gemboree is one of the region's largest mineral shows. This heritage contributes to Franklin's tourism economy.

Is there unrestricted land available in Franklin?

Yes, Franklin and Macon County have significant amounts of unrestricted land available, often at lower prices than neighboring counties. Large acreage parcels suitable for homesteading, farming, or off-grid living are available. As always, county building codes, septic permits, and setback requirements still apply. See our unrestricted land guide for details.

How close is Franklin to the Appalachian Trail?

The Appalachian Trail passes through Macon County, with the trail crossing at Winding Stair Gap about 10 miles south of Franklin on US-64. Franklin is a popular resupply stop for AT thru-hikers. Standing Indian Mountain and the Nantahala National Forest provide additional hiking opportunities minutes from town.

What are property tax rates in Macon County?

Macon County's property tax rate is approximately $0.32 per $100 of assessed value, making it one of the lower rates in Western NC. Properties within Franklin town limits pay an additional municipal tax. For current rates, visit the Macon County Tax Office.

How far is Franklin from Asheville?

Franklin is approximately 65 miles southwest of Asheville, about a 1 hour and 15 minute drive via US-23/441. The route passes through Sylva and Dillsboro. Highlands and Cashiers are about 25-30 minutes to the southeast. Atlanta, GA is approximately 2.5 hours south.
